# Historical manual curation

The manual review was performed on 15 October 2026 and updated on 24 October 2026. The chronology and subsequent changes are recorded below.

Why genes were removed from the original comparison
Removing HLA-C, HLA-DRB1, SLC22A1, IZUMO4 and PRB3 reduced the original set from 17 to 12 gene assignments. Removing CYP1A1 and MYO15A reduced it to ten. AP3D1, MOB3A, SERPINA1 and PIEZO1 were subsequently removed from those ten, leaving six genes from Daniel’s original set. ARL9 and MROH2A were separate removals from the earlier shortlist; they were not members of Daniel’s original 17. The individual rationales follow.
HLA C
HLA-C encodes a class I antigen-presenting molecule that also interacts with natural-killer-cell KIR receptors. The three selected records were two heterozygous missense calls and one homozygous missense call. HLA loci are exceptionally polymorphic, and conventional alignment against a single reference can misrepresent divergent haplotypes; the calls require HLA-aware interpretation before isolated consequence scores are trusted. The review excluded this gene from the principal constitutional MVA ranking because these particular calls did not provide a coherent allele-level explanation for chromosome instability, rhabdomyosarcoma and the renal phenotype. This is not an assertion of no reproductive connection: maternal KIR and parental HLA-C combinations have been associated with recurrent miscarriage. That family-level hypothesis would require parental HLA/KIR analysis and is different from assigning causality to the child’s three SNVs. [52,54,55]
HLA DRB1
HLA-DRB1 encodes the beta chain of a class II antigen-presentation molecule, principally relevant to professional antigen-presenting cells. The output contained five distinct records, including apparent splice-disrupting, frameshift and stop annotations. Their number and severity are not independent evidence for a constitutional loss-of-function syndrome in this complex locus. Highly divergent HLA haplotypes, related DRB genes and transcript/reference choices make these records especially dependent on locus-specific validation. The review excluded HLA-DRB1 because the available variants and known immune biology did not establish a unifying mechanism for this child’s presentation. Immune susceptibility associations alone did not supply that missing link. [53,54]
SLC22A1
SLC22A1 encodes OCT1, an organic-cation transporter with an important hepatic role in uptake and disposition of drugs and endogenous compounds. Its selected record, chr6:160139865 CTGGTAAGT>C, was called homozygous and annotated as a splice-donor variant, with CADD 22.8. A potentially altered transporter could be pharmacogenetically relevant, but that does not establish a cause of mosaic aneuploidy or the combined childhood cancer, growth and renal phenotype. The review excluded it from the disease-causal shortlist because the transport mechanism and this allele lacked case-specific causal support; The review did not establish that it was biologically inert or irrelevant to drug handling. [56]
IZUMO4
IZUMO4 belongs to the IZUMO family, studied in reproductive biology; the precise human physiological role of IZUMO4 is less resolved than the sperm–egg fusion role established for IZUMO1. The two candidates were chr19:2097949 C>T, annotated as Arg131Cys with CADD 23.5, and chr19:2098981 G>GCTC, an in-frame insertion without CADD. The review excluded the gene because neither allele had verified evidence connecting it to constitutional chromosome instability or the child’s multisystem phenotype. Family membership cannot establish such a mechanism, and the child’s genotype cannot by itself explain the parents’ reproductive history. These same physical variants also generated flanking-gene proposals for AP3D1 and MOB3A, considered separately below. [57]
PRB3
PRB3 encodes a secreted salivary proline-rich protein. The candidate was a homozygous 126-base deletion at chr12:11267457, annotated as an in-frame deletion in a repetitive protein segment, without CADD or frequency in the six groups. Its saliva-associated function, repetitive sequence context and absence of a demonstrated relevant molecular effect gave it little support as a cause of the full phenotype. It was removed from the main ranking; its missing annotations were not themselves the reason for exclusion. [58]
CYP1A1
CYP1A1 is a xenobiotic-metabolizing cytochrome P450 enzyme that can bioactivate environmental compounds, so a broad connection with carcinogen biology is real. The two selected missense calls were chr15:74720638 G>T Arg464Ser, CADD 24.0, and chr15:74722722 C>T Asp126Asn, CADD 26.4. However, a possible change in exposure-dependent metabolism did not explain a constitutional chromosome-instability phenotype with prenatal growth and renal abnormalities. The review therefore excluded CYP1A1 from the principal causal ranking rather than interpreting cancer-association literature as proof that these two inherited alleles caused the child’s disorder. [59]
MYO15A
MYO15A encodes myosin XVa, an actin-based motor required for growth and maintenance of inner-ear hair-cell stereocilia. Its established recessive disease mechanism primarily produces hereditary hearing loss. The proposed pair comprised chr17:18130805 A>T Ile1345Leu, CADD 24.9, and chr17:18161408 C>T Leu3160Phe, CADD 22.0. Despite the possibility of a two-allele configuration, neither the known mechanism nor the available allele evidence provided an explanation for the tumour, nephrocalcinosis and growth phenotype. The gene was removed from this main disease ranking on that basis. [60]
AP3D1
AP3D1 encodes the delta subunit of adaptor complex AP-3, which sorts cargo to lysosomes and related organelles. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause Hermansky–Pudlak syndrome type 10, with immunodeficiency and neurological involvement. Crucially, our two chr19 candidates were annotated as downstream of AP3D1, not as AP3D1 coding or canonical splice changes. The script could pair them because it retained flanking annotations. Neither a demonstrated regulatory effect on AP3D1 nor a fitting AP3D1 disease mechanism was established, so The review excluded this proposal. The reason is stronger than a general phenotype mismatch: the purported target gene itself was not demonstrated. [61,67]
MOB3A
MOB3A is a regulator of kinase signaling related to the Hippo pathway. Experiments with hMOB3 proteins support an interaction with MST1 and effects on apoptosis and glioblastoma growth, so a cell-growth connection should not be dismissed. However, the same two chr19 variants were only upstream annotations for MOB3A. Their effects on MOB3A expression, protein abundance or signaling were not established. The review excluded MOB3A because flanking proximity plus a general tumour-pathway association was insufficient to attribute these specific alleles to the child’s disease; the proposal did not identify an independently verified coding pair in MOB3A. [62,67]
SERPINA1
SERPINA1 encodes alpha-1 antitrypsin, produced predominantly by hepatocytes and secreted to protect tissues against proteases. Pathogenic alleles can produce liver disease through abnormal protein accumulation and lung disease through insufficient antiprotease activity. Our four apparent frameshift indels resolve into two locally phased cis groups: chr14:94379487 CCT>C with 94379492 G>GAC, and 94379541 G>GA with 94379548 C>CTA. Their net length changes are zero and three bases respectively, so the reading frame can be restored downstream; the combined haplotype must be annotated before treating each isolated frameshift as an independent null allele. This does not prove a normal protein. Given that complication and the weak match to the combined MVA phenotype, The review excluded SERPINA1 from the main causal review. [63]
ARL9
ARL9 encodes an ARF-like small GTPase. Such proteins can act as nucleotide-dependent molecular switches, but the specific physiological partners and functions of ARL9 remain incompletely characterized. The retained earlier allele, chr4:56523792 CCTGA>C, was a heterozygous frameshift with CADD 32 and grpmax 0.008819%. Thus this exclusion was not caused by a low CADD score or excessive frequency. The review excluded ARL9 because no established allele-specific or human disease mechanism connected this variant with the child’s chromosome instability, renal disease and tumour presentation. Expression associations in other cancers would not settle that question. [64]
MROH2A
MROH2A encodes a HEAT-repeat-containing protein, a structural architecture often used for protein interactions; RefSeq still describes its function as uncharacterized. The earlier candidate, chr2:233807853 C>T, was a heterozygous stop-gained variant with CADD 34 and grpmax 0.01443%. It therefore deserved attention technically, but the biological review did not verify an established MROH2A syndrome resembling MVA or a functional result for this allele. Exploratory interaction or proteomic observations did not establish membership in a validated growth-regulation complex. The review excluded it because its current causal support was weaker than that of the retained candidates, not because a high-impact annotation was considered unimportant. [65]
PIEZO1
PIEZO1 encodes a mechanically activated ion channel involved in vascular and lymphatic biology; pathogenic variants can cause disorders including recessive lymphatic dysplasia, while other mechanisms affect red-cell hydration. Thus developmental and perinatal connections are plausible at the gene level. In our output, however, the PIEZO1 proposal reused chr16:88714226 AGGTGTG>A, the same homozygous deletion annotated in CTU2. For PIEZO1 it was downstream, not a demonstrated PIEZO1 coding or splice lesion. The review excluded the PIEZO1 gene assignment because no regulatory mechanism linked this deletion to PIEZO1. The physical variant remains among the 33 through CTU2, so deleting this gene association did not delete the CTU2 record. [66,67]
How the final set of 29 genes was assembled
The earlier review document contained 30 distinct variants. Removing RBMY2TP and the noncoding chr20:29878059 C>CTA insertion left 28. Removing the ARL9 and MROH2A variants left 26 variants assigned to 25 primary genes. The six surviving genes from Daniel’s original set were ASAP3, BUB1B, CTU2, IGBP1, MAD2L1BP and ZNF418. BUB1B and CTU2 were already among those 25; ASAP3, IGBP1, MAD2L1BP and ZNF418 added four new gene targets. Thus the union contains 29 genes.
That gene union is ACLY, ADAMTS1, ASAP3, BUB1B, CD82, CITED2, CNOT10, CTU2, DIDO1, FRZB, IGBP1, LRP4, LZTR1, MAD2L1BP, MEP1B, PIK3C3, PRMT6, ROCK1, SIM2, SLC8A1, SMC4, SOCS5, SVIL, TACC2, THUMPD3, TRDMT1, TSSK6, ZNF197 and ZNF418. These are primary target assignments for this review. Other overlapping or nearby VEP symbols were not silently added as extra ranked genes.
The search that produced 33 variants
The search started from Lucia’s 2429 CADD-scored variants plus her 68 variants without CADD, totaling 2497 distinct records. For the unscored keys, The review retrieved genotype and allele-matched annotations from Daniel’s normalized Filter 1 VCF. The review searched every record in this source cohort for the 29 target genes, retaining their relevant intragenic and upstream/downstream associations and the previously used MAD2L1BP window assignment. This was not a search of every variant in the unfiltered whole-genome VCF. [68,69,75]
The frequency rule removed records whose maximum observed AF exceeded 5% in AFR, AMR, EAS, MID, NFE or SAS. A record with at least one available group frequency was tested against the largest available value; it was retained for missing frequency only if all six group fields were empty. AMI, ASJ, FIN and REMAINING were not included in this particular maximum. This reproduces the group selection discussed with Daniel and avoids relying on a diluted global average. The 5% threshold is a permissive partner-search screen, not a claim that an allele near that frequency could explain an ultra-rare, fully penetrant disorder. [42]
Across the complete source cohort, the AF screen retained 401 scored and 19 unscored records, or 420. Searching the 29 targets within those records retained 30 with CADD and three without it. All 30 scored target variants have CADD strictly greater than 20, so the requested CADD >20 or missing rule gives these same 33 target variants. The frequency implementation used ≤5%; none of the 33 has AF exactly 5%, so using <5% gives the same target set. No additional DP or GQ cutoff was applied.
Among the 33, 16 have at least one available frequency in the six groups and 17 do not. There are 31 records with a direct VEP gene-symbol association to a target, including one downstream ASAP3 deletion; 30 have an intragenic target annotation. The remaining two are associated with MAD2L1BP only through its window. CADD is a prediction score that integrates annotations; a missing score does not make the sequence harmless or damaging. [41,43]
The final 33 comprise 26 variants retained from the earlier 30 plus seven additions: two ASAP3 variants, IGBP1, ZNF418, the two MAD2L1BP-window insertions and the additional ADAMTS1 deletion at chr21:26844275. Removing a gene assignment was not always equivalent to removing a physical variant, as illustrated by the CTU2/PIEZO1 record.
Which genes can form proposals
Seven genes have either two heterozygous candidates or a GT 1/1 call in this selected set. Only five fit the autosomal trans, unknown-phase pair or homozygous categories. ADAMTS1 adds a cis pair, and IGBP1 is an X-chromosome case. This distinction keeps the arithmetic explicit while preserving the different inheritance interpretations.
The other 22 genes each have exactly one associated heterozygous variant among these 33 and no second retained variant annotated to that gene as intragenic, upstream or downstream. They are ACLY, CD82, CITED2, CNOT10, DIDO1, FRZB, LRP4, LZTR1, MEP1B, PIK3C3, PRMT6, ROCK1, SIM2, SLC8A1, SMC4, SOCS5, SVIL, TACC2, THUMPD3, TRDMT1, TSSK6 and ZNF197. This statement is restricted to the 33; it does not rule out earlier-filtered, structural, regulatory or undetected second alleles in the child’s genome.
Inspection of all 26375 Filter 1 records found additional, often common variants in several loci. One surviving call therefore must not be described as the only variant in the entire gene. A putative second hit also needs a credible effect on that gene.


## Corrections on 24 October
The MAD2L1BP window association is no longer accepted. Both window-only insertions were removed. Additional candidates retained after review: LRP4 chr11:46878979 T>C (LR2) and TACC2 chr10:122194203 CA>C (T18). Both relevant pairs have unknown phase. The baseline matrix contains 31 variants in 28 genes after removing the two window insertions. LR2 and T18 are separate later-review additions and do not alter the group ranking.
